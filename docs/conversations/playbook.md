# Converting to customer — decisions & playbook

Standing decisions about how a Liid conversation is run, and the reasoning behind each. This is the
distillate of [the conversation log](README.md); the conversations are the evidence, this is the
conclusion.

**Evidence level: thin.** As of 2026-08-10 this is built on one logged conversation plus outside
benchmarks. Rules marked **[decided]** I intend to hold regardless. Rules marked **[provisional]**
are one-deal inferences that the next few conversations should confirm or kill. Don't treat
provisional rules as settled — the point of logging is to find out where they're wrong.

---

## 1. How to quote

**[decided] Never quote two segments as one number.** Price the narrow, cheap, obviously-worth-it
list on its own. A ~250€ decision and a ~3000€ decision are not the same conversation, and stapling
them together turns an easy yes into "kallis". If someone names several lists, pick the smallest one
with the clearest payback and quote only that.

**[provisional] Lead with the batch total, not the €/contact unit.** A per-contact price invites
comparison to contact databases, where a contact costs cents — an axis Liid loses on 100% of the
time no matter how much better the data is. The industry benchmark for this category is
cost-per-meeting (€150–600 mainstream B2B) or cost-per-client. *Open question: does quoting
"280 kontakti, 280€" instead of "1€/kontakt" actually change the reaction? Needs 2–3 more quotes to
tell.*

**[decided] Never volunteer a competitor's name into a price conversation.** Naming a cheaper-feeling
alternative to a price-sensitive buyer hands them the comparison. Only discuss a competitor they
named first.

## 2. Handling "too expensive"

**[decided] Diagnose before responding. Ask one question and stop.** "Kallis võrreldes millega?" —
then nothing. The value of the question is the silence after it. Answering it yourself destroys it.

**[decided] Separate the two halves of the objection.** People rarely say only one thing:

| They said | It means | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "aitaks ikka, **aga** kallis" | value conceded, commitment size is the problem | shrink the scope, don't argue value |
| "ei tea kas aitaks, ja kallis" | value not established | back to demo/needs, price is downstream |
| a specific counter-number | real negotiation | decide if it's a deal |
| "kallis" + no number, twice asked | not a buyer | park, don't chase |

Arguing value against a conceded-value objection is answering a question nobody asked.

**[decided] Restructure the scope, never cut the unit price.** Same €/kontakt, smaller first batch.
A discount says the original number was fake and resets every future negotiation with that person.
Shrinking scope keeps the price honest *and* lowers the total — it does the same job without the cost.

**[decided] Be willing to lose it.** If the smallest sensible batch is still "kallis", price was never
the objection. Park with a date, move on. Chasing teaches nothing and costs the next deal's attention.

## 3. Channel and cadence

**[decided] Match their effort level.** A five-word replier gets a reply they can answer in one word.
Open-ended questions to low-effort correspondents return nothing.

**[decided] One unanswered ask is a signal.** Ignoring "mis su number on?" is a channel preference,
not an oversight. Don't re-ask for a call — meet them where they're replying.

**[provisional] Make the next reply a yes/no, not homework.** Prefer "Alustame ainult sellest? Ca N
kontakti, N€. Sobib?" over "mis su eelarve on?". Ask the open question as a fallback line at the end,
never as the main lift. *Watch whether this actually beats asking for the number outright.*

## 4. Where deals actually leak

Ordered by how much they cost, worst first. All three below came out of one conversation — treat the
ordering as a hypothesis.

1. **First-touch positioning.** Markus signed up and still asked whether sending and email-writing
   were on the roadmap. Someone who reaches the pricing conversation with the wrong mental model of
   the product will price it wrong. *(Site since rewritten — recheck this on the next few signups.)*
2. **The quote itself.** See §1. More deals are probably lost to how the number was assembled than to
   the number.
3. **Automation touching live manual threads.** Sequence steps hitting a person mid-conversation
   costs an apology and the thread's momentum. *(Fixed 2026-08.)*

## 5. Open questions

Things the log should answer once there's more than one conversation in it.

- Does the batch-total quote outperform the €/contact quote?
- What's the actual floor? Is there a total below which nobody says "kallis" — and is that number a
  price or a *count*?
- Do buyers who concede value ("aitaks ikka") convert at a materially higher rate than the ones who
  never say it? If so, that sentence is the qualifying signal and the demo's job is to produce it.
- Does the smaller-first-batch close actually lead to the bigger second batch, or does it cap the deal?
- Is the demo video doing its job, or is it where people go quiet?

## Objections

### "Aitaks ikka aga hind sutsuke kallis"

**What it actually says.** Two separate facts, and it's worth not blurring them:

- *"Aitaks ikka"* — **value is conceded.** He's not saying he doesn't see the point. This is not a
  value-demonstration problem, and any reply that argues value is answering a question he didn't ask.
- *"Sutsuke"* — a smidge. Not "way out of range", not "no budget". Combined with the fact that he
  never named a counter-number even when asked, this reads as **commitment size**, not affordability:
  he's looking at one large total and flinching.

**What made the total large.** I quoted both lists at once. Segment 2 (Lõuna-Eesti maaklerid) is
~200–300 contacts ≈ ~250€. Segment 1 ("suuremad ettevõtted kõikidest riikidest") is thousands of
contacts and thousands of euros. **The shock is almost certainly segment 1, and I attached it to a
deal that segment 2 could have closed on its own.**

**My first draft (not sent).** Asked *"Kallis võrreldes millega?"* and then immediately answered it
myself with two benchmarks — cheap international contact DB, and Äripäev's list. Killed for three
reasons:

1. Answering my own diagnostic question destroys it. The value of "compared to what?" is the silence
   after it; he has to name the benchmark. Supplying benchmarks gives him a free exit.
2. It **names Äripäev to a price-sensitive buyer who hadn't mentioned it.** Never volunteer a
   competitor into a price objection.
3. It ended on two open questions (his alternative *and* his target price). This man replies in five
   words. Two open questions to a low-effort replier returns zero answers — and I'd already burned
   one unanswered ask on him (the phone number, 07-10).

**What I'm sending instead** — restructure the scope, hold the unit price, make the ask answerable in
one word, put the price question in as the fallback rather than the lift:

> Tänud. Kuule aga ma pakkusin sulle mõlemad nimekirjad korraga ära, see teebki suure numbri.
>
> Lõuna-Eesti maaklerid on kordades väiksem tükk kui "üritustekorraldajad kõikidest riikidest".
> Alustame ainult sellest? Ca [N] kontakti, [N]€. Näed reaalse tulemuse ära ja siis otsustad edasi.
>
> Kui asi pole selles, siis ütle lihtsalt number mis sul mõttes oli.

**Read on the answer:**

- *"Jah, alustame"* → customer. Segment 1 becomes the natural upsell once the maaklerid batch produces replies.
- *He names a number* → real negotiation, decide if it's a deal.
- *~250€ is still "kallis"* → it was never price. Not a buyer. Park until autumn, don't chase.

No discount was offered and none should be. Same €/kontakt, smaller scope.

## What I learned

- **The €/kontakt unit is a positioning liability.** It benchmarks Liid against contact databases,
  where a contact costs cents — an axis I lose on 100% of the time regardless of quality. Lead-gen is
  benchmarked on cost-per-meeting (€150–600 mainstream B2B) or cost-per-client. Worth thinking about
  whether the quote should lead with a batch total and an implied cost-per-reply instead of a unit price.
- **Never quote two segments in one number.** Price the narrow, cheap, obviously-worth-it segment
  alone. A ~250€ decision and a ~3000€ decision are not the same conversation.
- **Him asking whether I "plan to add" sending and writing (07-02) was a landing-page failure**, not a
  him failure — the site did not communicate that the product already sends. *(Fixed since; the site
  has been rewritten.)* Keep the lesson: a first-touch positioning leak costs more than any single deal.
- **He never gave a phone number when asked.** Treat that as a channel preference, not a miss.
  Don't re-ask for a call.
- The 07-15/07-17 sequence emails re-hit a live manual conversation and cost me an apology email on
  07-20. *(Fixed since.)*

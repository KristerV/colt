# Sales conversation log

One file per prospect conversation. The point is **not** to duplicate the CRM — the app already
stores the thread. The point is to capture what the thread *doesn't*: what the objection actually
was, what I tried, why, and what it taught me about the offer, the pricing and the ICP.

**[playbook.md](playbook.md)** is the distillate — the standing decisions on how to quote, how to
handle "too expensive", and where deals leak. Read it before a pricing conversation. Read the
Patterns section below before scoping a pricing change or rewriting the pitch.

## File naming

`YYYY-MM-DD-firstname-lastname.md` — the date is **first contact**, not last. Files sort into the
order I met people, which is the order the lessons arrived in.

## Template

```markdown
# <Name> — <company / what they do>

- **First contact:** YYYY-MM-DD (how they arrived: signup / cold / referral)
- **Status:** open | won | lost | parked (YYYY-MM-DD)
- **Segment:** what kind of buyer this is
- **Asked for:** the lists/outcome they said they wanted, in their words

## Timeline
Dated bullets. Facts only, no interpretation. Quote their actual words for anything that matters —
paraphrase loses the signal.

## Objections
One heading per objection. Their exact words, what it actually meant, what I did, what happened.

## What I learned
The transferable part. Offer, pricing, positioning, ICP. This is what gets promoted to Patterns.

## Next
One line. What happens next and when.
```

Quote people verbatim. `"Aitaks ikka aga hind sutsuke kallis"` carries information — "he said it was
too expensive" throws that information away.

### Watching for
Candidate patterns from a single conversation. Confirm or kill on the next deal.

- **Quoting a per-contact unit invites the wrong comparison.** €/kontakt benchmarks against contact
  databases, where the price is cents. The industry quotes cost-per-meeting for a reason. — Markus
- **Quoting two segments at once creates fake sticker shock.** One big total for a wide list plus a
  narrow list reads as "expensive" when the narrow list alone was an easy yes. Quote the small
  segment first, alone. — Markus
- **Low-effort repliers won't take a call.** Ignoring "mis su number on?" is a signal, not an
  oversight. Meet them in email and make the next reply answerable in one word. — Markus
